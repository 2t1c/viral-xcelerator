# Digital Products — Build Once, Sell Forever

The cleanest way to turn attention into income. You make the product once and sell it infinitely: no inventory, no shipping, no cost per sale. Every sale after the first is almost pure margin.

For a content account it is the natural fit. Your content already pulls in the exact audience for the product, and the product gives your posts a reason to drive clicks instead of just likes.

This course runs in four sections: Foundation, Ideation, Creation, Launch.

## Foundation — Why a Digital Product

You make it once and sell it forever. One free product quietly builds an email list and a buyer base for years.

**The math that makes it obvious:**
- A post drives roughly 1 click per 1,000 views to your link.
- Each click is worth your EPC (earnings per click).
- Example from the course: a free mental-models product ran at an EPC of about $0.24.
- 500 clicks at $0.24 is about $120 from one post. Run volume and it compounds.

**Why we lead with free.** Most beginners try to sell a $50 product to a cold audience and wonder why nobody buys. Do the opposite: lead with a free or pay-what-you-want product.

Free removes friction. People grab it, join your list, and the product sells itself once they own it. You upsell paid offers later to an audience that already trusts you.

## Ideation — Find What People Already Want

The biggest mistake with a first product is building something clever that nobody asked for. Do not invent demand. Find it, then serve it.

**Read the signals your audience already sends:**
- Questions you get repeatedly in replies, DMs, and comments.
- Your best-performing posts. A topic that went viral is a topic people want more of.
- What competitors already sell. If it sells in your niche, demand is proven.
- What people already pay to solve in your space.

If three people ask the same question, that is a product.

**Validate before you build:**
- Post about the topic and watch engagement.
- Run a simple "comment X and I'll send it" offer and count the comments.
- Check that similar products have reviews and sales, not just exist.

**The shortcut:** take your single best-performing piece of content and expand it into the deeper, organized version. The post already proved the demand.

Aim for the overlap between what your audience clearly wants, what you can credibly create, and what is simple enough to ship fast.

## Creation — Build It in About 30 Minutes with AI

You can paste a lesson straight into your AI and have it walk you through the build. The course build runs in five stages. Full prompts live in the Notion lesson and in `prompts-library/`.

1. **Discovery.** Map your audience's real pains, then pick a sharp angle. Do not try to be original on the topic, be original on the angle. "Incentives" is a tired topic. "Incentives as the meta-skill underneath every other thinking framework" is a fresh angle.
2. **Brand foundation.** Lock a voice, an aesthetic, and a 4-color palette before you write. This is where beginner products fail: the writing is fine but the product feels generic, so it gets treated as generic. Build the brand system once and reuse it across every product.
3. **Structure.** Outline 3-5 parts and 15-25 short chapters. Target 30-50 pages so the reader finishes in one or two sittings. Cut your first outline by 20% before you write. Completion is what drives word-of-mouth.
4. **Writing.** Write one chapter at a time with a strong template. Nail chapter 1 and heavily refine it, because it becomes the voice anchor for everything after. Re-anchor when the voice drifts.
5. **Naming.** The title is your single most important sales asset. The best titles borrow authority from a known idea, then add a sharp subtitle that states the actual promise.

**Increase perceived value.** Copy, cover, and aesthetic are what make a free product feel worth paying for. A tight, well-designed product beats an ambitious one you never launch.

## Launch — Where to Sell It and How to Drive Clicks

**Default platform: Gumroad.** Free to start, supports free / paid / pay-what-you-want, and handles checkout, delivery, and email capture. It gives you one link you drop straight into your posts.

Other options: Stan Store (link-in-bio storefront for several offers), your own site (once established), Notion shared link (free templates). Do not overthink it. Start on Gumroad and move later if you outgrow it.

**Quick setup:** create the product, set pricing (free or pay-what-you-want to start), write the listing with the Gumroad description prompt, add the cover, grab your link. That link is what every CTA points to.

**Writing the CTA.** The thread earns the attention, the CTA converts it. Lead with the free product so the click is frictionless, build the list, and upsell paid offers later. Match the CTA to the emotion of the thread and place it before your best media so it is seen.

## The one rule for your first product

Lead with free or pay-what-you-want. Free removes friction, builds the list, and warms the audience for everything you sell after.
