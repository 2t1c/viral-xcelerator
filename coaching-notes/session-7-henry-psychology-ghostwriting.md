# Session 7 — Henry: Psychology/Philosophy Ghostwriting Deep-Dive

**Context:** 1-on-1 with Henry, an experienced ghostwriter moving into the rare psychology/philosophy niche (market is ~80% health, ~5–10% business/finance, a tiny slice psychology). Built around a prep doc of timeless hook templates. Aim: teach the *underlying principles* so he can replicate everything without you. This is the most comprehensive session — much of `fundamentals/` and `swipe-files/` derives from it.

## The thread landscape & new algorithm
- **Evolution:** pre-2023 OG writers didn't use threads to grow; ~Feb–Mar 2023 viral-thread pages shifted the format from "5 lessons" lists to informative/educational/storytelling threads. **May–Aug 2025 = golden age.**
- Two big shifts: (1) the **bar for hooks went through the roof** — every word must fight for its life (more competition for the same/less attention, incl. theme pages stealing threads); (2) threads need a **stronger narrative + clearer outcome**.
- **The 3-month gap:** X killed threads, lost readers (people came specifically to read them — the long-form-YouTube equivalent), realized the mistake, brought them back. Expect **more organic reach over 6–12 months**, retweets becoming less essential — but for now you still need them for the initial push.
- **Benchmarks:** insane >500k · good ~50–200k · horrifying <10k. Simple one/two-line + carousel hooks now hit big numbers (lower-end audience, less reading friction).
- **Gray area:** even >5% CTR and >50% retention no longer guarantee a boost (algo in flux, X using Grok). So **first principles: great hook + enough distribution. Control the controllables.**

## Success metrics
- **CTR (>5%)** and **retention (>50%)** remain fundamental.
- A hook is **an offer** — Hormozi value equation: (perceived value × likelihood) ÷ (effort × time). The hook must answer *why should they care?*
- **Internal engagement:** comments resurface threads; controversial threads spark conversation — reply after posting to keep it going. **QRT** something in the hook to borrow another post's traffic (free, no downside).
- **Bookmark ratio:** secondary signal of perceived value. Not every hook is built to be bookmarked — some to be *opened* (curiosity), some to *spark comments* (controversy). Best combine all three.

## The Risk-o-meter
"Everyone's an artist until rent is due." Threads are performative marketing — with a client (rent), tune *down* creativity toward proven templates.
- **Own pages = playground** (risk up): test ideas/templates so you learn what's proven.
- **Client work = risk 1–2:** start from a proven template, mold it, add the right keywords. You need results.
- At peak: **~150 threads/month** (≈80–100 own, ~50 client) with two helpers — every post got a "how much does this *need* to perform, and what's the cost if it flops?" check.

## Defining a "genius" thread
- **Basics:** 12–18 tweets, <280 chars, narrative, listicles, cliffhangers, ≤2 tweets without media.
- **Genius layer:** tailor visuals, hook style, and a **per-client viral-phrase bank** to *one* envisioned reader. Build a **client repository** (story, voice, word bank, preferred style, length).
- **Learn the client** by scraping everything online + GPT extraction (you haven't done a formal interview in over a year); fully dialed in over ~2–3 threads.
- **Unfair advantage:** mine peak (credentials) and lowest point (for before/after) + **authority association** (books, people). Small wins work if framed impressively — numbers game ("without X," "I used to be X but now Y") or short two-liner route. Don't post low MRR figures on X.
- **Placeholder method:** when the body needs the client's transformation/insight you lack, drop a placeholder ("add your experience") — they fill, you edit. Use sparingly. **Reusable components:** well-written bridges/CTAs in the client's voice can be saved and reused.

## Hierarchy of success: Idea > Hook > Distribution > Body
1. **Idea (#1):** ~5× harder to write a good hook from a bad idea. A good idea stops the scroll in one line. Choose *proven* ideas (performed somewhere) + an **unexpected angle** on a known person/concept. If *you* find it interesting, you write it better.
2. **Hook (#2):** tap proven formulas for certainty, or be creative within them. Keep **85–90% structural similarity** when you need results (5% too loose, 95% risks being called out).
3. **Distribution (#3):** ~$40–50/day ≈ removes it from the equation; beginners ~$15–20. Use a **wholesaler** (one person spreads it).
4. **Body (#4):** the one job — **deliver on the promise made in the hook** (its last line). Close the curiosity loop — only clickbait if you don't deliver. Listicles always work; story-as-listicle and internal listicles too. Comes back to the outline.

## Psychology = the sweet spot
The mix of health and business: health's powerful universal keywords (anxiety, stress, depression — used as a wide net) + business's proven structure. Two types: storytelling and conceptual.

## The Inner-CTA ("never seen anyone teach this on X")
The **last line of the hook reframes the whole thread.** Same story, but "the pattern I witnessed blew my mind" / "his last words left me thinking" instead of generic "here's how." Changes how people *perceive* it → a **blue ocean**, a category of one.
- **How:** find the most interesting point in the thread, then **ask the question that sits just before it** — that framed question becomes the CTA line.
- These lines are still partly proven viral phrases, so risk is lower than it looks.

## Negotiable vs Non-Negotiable (the house analogy)
A template = a house. **Non-negotiables = frame/foundation** (remove and it collapses). **Negotiables = furniture** (the info you swap). Bracket the negotiables; don't touch the non-negotiables (or change only slightly, via synonym). Keep the **first line broad** for top-of-funnel; concentrate value in the one "prime real estate" line.

## What-Why-How (the universal spine)
Every viral hook (and whole thread) follows **What → Why → How.** What = one big idea; **Why = the heavy lifting** (why should I care?); How = lessons/story/steps. Psychology makes the "why" easy; **business is the hardest niche** (must manufacture relevance).

## The contrast principle (business)
The gap between outcome and starting point/effort drives intrigue. Pizza boy → $1.3B on $5; "new Steve Jobs" buys a struggling $5-tier Welsh club for $2.5M. Bigger contrast = more intriguing.

## Template walk-through highlights
- *"This VC firm can see the future"* (Sequoia) — conceptual; lean on authority association (3 or 5 names, never 4; 7 too many). End with definitive numbers.
- *"Ryan Reynolds is the new Steve Jobs"* (21M views) — "X is the new Y" + one make-or-break setup sentence; build the narrative around the one idea.
- *"The youngest billionaire you've never heard of"* — swap one keyword but keep the same level of power; one word re-positions the thread.
- Brian Meyerhoffer's conceptual/health-psychology hooks — placeholders + a recurring proven phrase ("nervous system"), tying health *benefits* into psychology naturally.

## Aesthetic / thumbnails
Premium = clean, expensive-feeling, considered color contrast. Blurry video or obvious AI image reads as lower quality. Match thumbnail emotion to the hook.

## Henry's approach + the warm-up
He reverse-engineers a similar hook's structure (claim → authority → specific CTA) or writes from scratch (often fails). Guidance: high similarity is a *starting* scaffold; a **warm-up** (read many hooks, underline keywords) primes the brain so even from-scratch hooks come easier; assemble with placeholders, pick the best of 2–3 against the intended narrative. **Volume tunes the "viral intuition."** (Recommended he follow up with Ondrej for more hook pattern-decoding.)

## Body, CTA & sign-off
- Treat the thread like a **sales letter** when promoting: context → bridge → problem → solution → action (CTA). Place CTA where interest peaks. **Hook = rigid; body & ending = flexible (your voice).**
- End with a punch: mission + about-me + "written by" + final quote; optional QRT or internal giveaway ("comment X, I'll DM you…") for leads.

## Paid distribution / native ads (less certain)
- Engagement groups mostly don't work on X now.
- **Promo button:** mixed. Boosted too early it can buy ~50k empty views (~$25/day) at near-zero CTR; a friend pushed a post to ~1M (~$30–40) → ~$1k in sales; your client John pushed an organic ~3M post to ~6M. **Timing unresolved** (boost after it's already gaining vs. near-instant). Worth experimenting — no downside, fast break-even.
- X may be cracking down on retweet vendors ("manipulating the platform" notices). Native promo may be the safer long-term path; ideal is X giving organic boost. Current algo metrics are weird/inconsistent — control post quality + distribution.

## Closing throughlines
- A hook is **Lego** — you hold the parts (or research them) and snap them together. Work creatively *within* the proven foundation, then graduate to fully unique hooks over a few months.
- "Every thread is an artwork" — the extra 5–10 min on a hook is skill-building, not waste.
- Theory ≠ application: post a lot, get reps, build intuition.
