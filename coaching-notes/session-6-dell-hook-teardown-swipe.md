# Session 6 — Dell: Hook Teardown & Swipe-File Logic

## Structure vs. information
- **Break every hook into STRUCTURE (keep) vs. INFORMATION (swap).** Bracket the info you'll change. Where you draw the brackets depends on confidence — keep a strong word if unsure what to replace it with.
- Example ("youngest billionaire you never heard of"): swapping one keyword changes the whole direction, but the replacement must carry the **same level of power/perceived value**.
- The **"prime real estate"** is the one line holding all the value — that's where the effort goes.
- **Viral phrases** ("once you see it, you can't unsee it") add little info but improve flow/look — optional, not mandatory.
- **Word choice signals value:** "strategies," "top executives," "study," "playbook" read premium vs. generic "here's how."
- **Story-specific hooks** (e.g. Stephen Hawking) are hard to reuse — only fit a similar setup. Start with the easy-to-adapt structures.

## Lean into conceptual / idea threads
Beyond story threads, do **idea/concept threads** — most ghostwriters won't, so it separates you. The big idea is one sentence (e.g. "water is the next monopoly for Wall Street"); everything else agitates and proves it matters. Works for AI, finance, even socio-political/economic angles (2026 recession, China–US) — gives the page a *position*, not just information.

## Build your own swipe file
Provided hooks are a starting set — when you find a good adaptable one, add it, bracket it, adapt your story to it. Build a categorized library (by use case), pin it in Typefully. Use Tim's AI prompts for research/inspiration, not as the core — the foundation is your proven-structure library.

## The high-level skill: fluent recall
Reading a new post, instantly recall past proven structures on the same theme, then borrow a proven first line. Search X for the known term/phrase to surface them. One proven line lowers risk and can make all the performance difference.

## Hook teardown — "Stanford AI chats were never private" (underperformed, ~4k views)

**Why it underperformed:** the two middle lines were **informative, not emotional** — they gave away information before the reader was made to care.

- **First line (big idea):** "Stanford just reviewed that your AI chats were never private" — clear, good **authority association** (Stanford). Bracket the time word ("just") — if the paper isn't recent it can't claim a breaking headline; swap to **"shocking"** so recency doesn't matter.
- **Lines 2–3 — the core lesson:** instead of explaining *how*, **make them care first** via importance or agitation. Get specific/sensationalize: "it knows you better than your partner… knows what you want to do next Friday" → vivid, fearful scene. Then agitate *again* on the next line ("but this is far from the worst part…").
- **Don't close the open loop early.** "They got the data by compiling docs from six AI companies" is informative filler that devalues the thread — move it into the body, not the hook.
- **Twist the knife with power words.** "Were never private" → "the **disturbing truth** about your AI chats." Collect keywords (*spy, surveilled, big tech selling your data*) into the swipe doc — Lego blocks for future hooks.
- **CTA line:** most flexible — emotional trigger or benefit-driven. Use power words; 1–2 clauses. Most thinking goes into the big-idea line, then the why-lines.

## Governing principle: Rule of One + What-Why-How
With so much noise, stick to **one idea, one concept, one action.** Hook = the **What**; next tweets = the **Why** (answer "why is this important?" well, or dig into the pain across the worst-case ↔ best-case spectrum); end = the **How**. Establish importance first, *then* give information.

## Metrics to judge a hook
- **Click-through rate** is the key signal (>5% = strong) and the main thing to optimize.
- **Bookmark-to-like ratio:** bookmarks higher than likes = high perceived value.
