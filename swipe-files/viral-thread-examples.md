# Viral Thread Examples — Curated Swipe File

**Purpose:** A hand-picked set of the highest-performing thread hooks we have studied. Each one cleared millions of views as the opening tweet of a real multi-tweet thread. Use them for voice, structure, and pivot language. Never copy a hook verbatim.

**Scope:** Hooks only (the first tweet). The body that followed is not reproduced here. Single tweets, quote-retweet fragments, and reply-bait were filtered out. What remains is curated, not dumped.

**How to read each entry:**
- The blockquote is the hook verbatim, exactly as it shipped.
- Stats are the public engagement on that opening tweet.
- The last line names the structural template so you can reuse the shape on a new topic.

## Patterns across the top 30

Counts are across the 32 curated hooks above. The five structures below recur the most.

**1. The explicit door closer (31 of 32).** Almost every hook ends on a promise that opens into the body. It lands as a colon, an ellipsis, or a "Here's..." line: "Here's how he did it:", "the rest is...", "15 lessons on communication:". The single most reliable rule in the set. A hook without a door underperforms.

**2. The specific number in the opener (30 of 32).** A precise figure does the scroll-stopping: "76 death matches", "1 in 7 adults", "$1.79 donation", "dropped by 30-50%", "228 IQ". Round or vague numbers are rare. The more oddly specific, the better it stops the thumb.

**3. The named anchor (24 of 32).** A person, brand, or institution the reader already recognizes: Statham, Apple, Spotify, Harvard, NASA, MIT, Zuckerberg, Milei. The familiar name buys the first second of attention, then the unexpected angle keeps it.

**4. The inversion or reframe (about 12 of 32).** "X isn't A, it's B" or "everyone thought X, but...": "Cancer isn't bad genes, it's damaged mitochondria", "ADHD isn't a disease, it's a superpower", "China spent $100M & everyone thought it was a waste." The hook sells the collision between belief and reality.

**5. The list or curation promise (about 14 of 32).** A countable reward: "15 American oddities", "24 uncomfortable truths", "8 foods", "7 ChatGPT prompts", "9 essential laws." Often the first item is shown inline so the reader sees the format before tapping.

### Power words that recur

Counted across the 32 hooks.

- **here's / here are** (the door): 13
- **greatest / most / finest / highest** (superlative): 12
- **proven / science / study / scientists** (credibility): 8
- **secret / hidden / banned / erased / concealed / afraid to discuss** (forbidden knowledge): 7
- **you / your** (direct address): 7
- **shocked / stunned / mindblowing / wild / bone-chilling** (intensity): 6
- **isn't / not / lied** (negation and inversion): 6

The takeaway: pair one specific number with one recognizable name, reframe a belief the reader holds, then end on an explicit door. That four-part move sits under most of the set.
