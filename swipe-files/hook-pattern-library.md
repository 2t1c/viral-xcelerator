# Hook Pattern Library

**Purpose:** Twelve hook templates, each pulled from threads that cleared millions of views. For every template you get the formula, a real verbatim example, when to reach for it, and the failure mode that kills it.

This file is the structural layer. For full verbatim hooks and their stats, see [viral-thread-examples.md](./viral-thread-examples.md). For the quantified word and metric breakdown, see [power-words-and-metrics.md](./power-words-and-metrics.md).

**Core spine.** Almost every template below resolves to the same four beats:

```
SETUP  →  STAKES  →  PIVOT  →  DOOR
who/what/when   scale or odds   the turn   the promise into the body
```

The templates are different ways to load that spine. Pick the one that fits the story, not the other way around.

---

## 1. The Negation Staircase

**Formula:** State the setup, then stack short negations that escalate the impossibility, then turn with "Then ONE..."

**Example (40.4M views):**
> In 1965, Singapore was forced out of Malaysia.
> No army.
> No resources.
> No fresh water.
> Then ONE man's ruthless vision built modern Asia's greatest success...

**When to use:** A rise-from-nothing story where the starting odds were brutal. The repetition does the emotional work.

**Failure mode:** Negations that do not escalate. "No army. No flag. No anthem." flattens because flag and anthem are not survival stakes. Each line must raise the stakes over the last.

---

## 2. The Inversion Reframe

**Formula:** Name the belief the reader holds, then break it in the same breath. "X isn't A. It's B."

**Example (26.8M views):**
> This is Thomas Seyfried. He's studied cancer for 30+ years.
> His message? Cancer isn't bad genes or bad luck, it's damaged mitochondria.
> This flips everything you've been told...

**When to use:** When your core insight contradicts the consensus. The hook sells the collision, not the topic.

**Failure mode:** The reframe is a nuance, not a contradiction. "Cancer isn't only genetic, it's also lifestyle" is a shrug. The B must genuinely displace the A.

---

## 3. The Outsider Gaze

**Formula:** Declare an identity, place it somewhere unfamiliar, then promise a numbered list of what stunned them.

**Example (63.2M views):**
> I'm European.
> I recently visited the USA for the first time since 2018.
> What I witnessed left me stunned.
> 15 American oddities I still can't wrap my head around:

**When to use:** Cultural or systemic observation. The outsider has permission to notice what locals stopped seeing.

**Failure mode:** A generic observer. "As a traveler" has no stakes. The identity must create a specific contrast with the subject.

---

## 4. The Forbidden Authority

**Formula:** Frame a person or method as suppressed by a trusted institution, name them, then promise their hidden lessons.

**Example (19.7M views):**
> The woman who heals what doctors can't:
> Barbara O'Neill.
> This 71-year-old naturopath says true healing comes from nature, not drugs.
> Here are her 9 essential laws...

**When to use:** Health, finance, and history where an underdog expert sits against a powerful establishment.

**Failure mode:** Naming no opponent. "Great health tips from an expert" has no tension. The hook needs the thing the authority is up against (doctors, Big Pharma, the textbook).

---

## 5. The Save-Bait Utility

**Formula:** Kill the incumbent tool, show a concrete before-and-after number, then promise the exact prompts or steps.

**Example (11.5M views):**
> R.I.P. GOOGLE FLIGHTS IN 2026.
> R.I.P. BOOKING DOT COM IN 2026.
> $1,412 flight. I paid $186.
> These 7 ChatGPT prompts quietly break airline pricing:

**When to use:** Tools, prompts, and tactics. This template wins bookmarks over likes because people save it to use later.

**Failure mode:** No concrete delta. "These prompts will save you money" is abstract. The "$1,412 to $186" jump is what makes the save feel mandatory. Always show the number.

## 6. The Unsolved Mystery

**Formula:** Open a loop that has no clean answer, anchor it with a name or exact date, then promise the deepest version of the story.

**Example (61.4M views):**
> More than 500,000 people disappear without a trace every year.
> In 2009, something even stranger happened: a man appeared.
> Peter Bergmann never existed.
> This is one of the greatest internet mystery of all time.

**When to use:** History, true crime, and unexplained phenomena. The unresolved gap is the engine.

**Failure mode:** Resolving the mystery in the hook. If the reader can guess the answer, the loop closes and they scroll. Withhold the payoff.

---

## 7. The Time-Jump Vindication

**Formula:** Big number plus "everyone thought it was a mistake," then jump years forward to the verdict that proves them wrong.

**Example (28.7M views):**
> China spent $100M on the 2008 Olympics opening ceremony & everyone thought it was a waste.
> But China saw something no one else did.
> 15 years later, it's considered the greatest investment in Olympic history.

**When to use:** Business and strategy stories where a contrarian bet paid off slowly.

**Failure mode:** No clear "everyone" to be wrong. The hook needs a named consensus to invert. Without the crowd's mistake, the vindication has no contrast.

---

## 8. The Numbered Truth List

**Formula:** Borrow authority (age, experience, a result), promise time saved, then deliver a counted list of hard lessons.

**Example (29.8M views):**
> I'm 54.
> I wasted 24 years learning this the hard way. But I'll teach you in 5 minutes.
> If you're soft, stop reading now.
> Here are 24 uncomfortable truths I wish I knew earlier:

**When to use:** Life lessons, self improvement, and any distilled-wisdom format. The dare ("if you're soft, stop reading") filters for the right reader and raises commitment.

**Failure mode:** Soft, generic truths. "Be kind and work hard" is a fridge magnet. The truths must be uncomfortable enough to risk disagreement.

---

## 9. The Hidden Detail in a Famous Thing

**Formula:** Name a revered object, zoom to one uncanny concrete detail, then promise that nothing is as it seems.

**Example (39.9M views):**
> This 600-year-old painting is one of the most mysterious in history.
> That mirror at the back is just 3 inches wide, yet it reflects the entire room in immense detail.
> Look closer and you'll realize nothing is as it seems...

**When to use:** Art, architecture, and cultural artifacts the reader has seen but never looked at closely.

**Failure mode:** Staying abstract. "It is full of hidden meaning" is a yawn. The "3 inches wide" specific is what makes the reader lean in.

---

## 10. The Bedroom-to-Empire Arc

**Formula:** "This is [name]," a failure-rate stat for their field, the turn, then the tiny origin detail that started it all.

**Example (43.6M views):**
> This is iShowSpeed.
> 99% of streamers fall off within a few months.
> But in just 3 years, Speed dethroned KSI, Logan Paul, and Adin Ross.
> And it all started with ONE $1.79 donation...

**When to use:** Founder and creator rise stories. The small concrete origin ("$1.79 donation") makes a huge outcome feel reachable.

**Failure mode:** Skipping the tiny detail. "He worked hard and made it" is invisible. The smallest, most specific starting point carries the hook.

---

## 11. The Suppressed Discovery

**Formula:** Exact timestamp, bulleted hard specifics, then the tag that someone powerful is hiding the answer.

**Example (8.26M views):**
> August 15, 1977: The night that changed astronomy forever.
> A signal from deep space at 10:16 PM:
> • Strength: 30x beyond human technology
> • Duration: 72 seconds
> • Origin: where no star exists
> NASA Scientists are still afraid to discuss what they found:

**When to use:** Science and history with a strange, documented event. The precision of the bullets sells the credibility.

**Failure mode:** Over-claiming past the evidence. If the bullets feel invented, the reader bounces. Keep the specifics verifiable and let the gap carry the intrigue.

---

## 12. The Paradox Proof

**Formula:** State a paradox flatly, name an unlikely source that proved it, then promise it will rewire how the reader sees everything.

**Example (5.66M views):**
> The most powerful people are the easiest to control.
> A Russian peasant proved this in 1916.
> Once you see it, you can't unsee it.
> And it explains everything about power:

**When to use:** Psychology and philosophy where a single principle reframes a whole domain.

**Failure mode:** A paradox that is just a contradiction with no resolution. The body has to actually deliver the mechanism, or the hook reads as a cheap tease.

---

## How to choose

1. Identify the one insight the thread proves. One, not three.
2. Match it to the template whose engine fits: mystery for an open loop, inversion for a consensus break, save-bait for a tool, time-jump for a slow bet.
3. Load the four-beat spine. Confirm there is a specific number, a recognizable anchor, and an explicit door.
4. Run the failure-mode check for that template before you ship.

If two templates fit, pick the one that lets you lead with the most specific concrete detail. Specificity is the through-line in every hook that worked.
