# Viral Thread in a Box

Your end-to-end system for turning one idea into a finished thread. Idea to first draft should take about 90 minutes once you're fluent.

The standalone prompts referenced here live in `prompts/`.

---

## 1/ Finding Ideas

**Method 1: Curate what you consume.**
- Pull from books, videos, and YouTube channels you already follow.
- For YouTube, pick long-form videos with 500k+ views. The views prove the idea resonates.
- Business channels to mine: The Invisible Hand, Moon, How Money Works, Humphrey Yang, Your Friend Andy, ColdFusion.

**Method 2: Start with a question.**
Most ideas come from one simple question: *Who are the best in the world at [topic]?* That opens up:
- What makes them so good? (patterns, case studies, lessons)
- Why can't others copy them? (mistakes, barriers)
- What's their story? (journey, conflict, turning points)

Do 5–10 minutes of quick research for direction, then run the research prompt.

## 2/ Research Prompt: Find Interesting Angles

This stage gathers raw material — don't synthesize yet. Keep a blank Notion page open for stray stats, connections, and quotes. Pull 1–2 articles and screenshot anything usable as media later.

Prompt: see `prompts/research-prompt.md`.

## 3/ Pick a Curiosity-Driven Outline

Choose one of three structures. Full skeletons in `prompts/outline-prompts.md`.

### Structure 1: Listicle in Narrative
A story told through a numbered list (e.g. the Japan "Lost Decades" thread). Best for rise-and-fall stories with clear cause-and-effect.

### Structure 2: Concept-Driven Storytelling
One bold concept carried through a story (e.g. the Mukesh Ambani / Jio takeover). Best for a single protagonist making one big, counterintuitive move.

### Structure 3: Bold Claim / Quote *(start here)*
Lead with a big claim, build authority, deliver a listicle, then bridge to a problem-solution (e.g. the Cathie Wood thread).

Why start here:
- Classic build-up that establishes authority and a big idea (T1–T2)
- A built-in listicle that makes writing easier (T3–T8)
- A simple problem → solution close (T10–T12)

```
T0 — HOOK
BUILD UP:    T1 — WHO IS {X}?   T2 — THE PREDICTION / BIG IDEA / BIG QUESTION
MAIN POINTS: T3–T7 — IDEA 1 through IDEA 5
PROBLEM→SOL: T8 — WHY THIS MATTERS   T9 — PROBLEM   T10 — OUR MODEL   T11 — WHY IT'S BETTER
WRAP UP:     T12 — AUTHOR INTRO   T13 — FOLLOW / CTA
```

Spend the most time expanding each tweet with follow-up questions. The better the outline, the better the draft.

## 4/ Write the First Draft

Replace placeholders with real info, one tweet at a time. Two ways to work:
1. Research 80% → write 80% → research + write the last 20% together
2. Research and write 100% as you go

Treat this as a **spiral of gradual improvement**. Each round you: (a) replace a placeholder, (b) add a new placeholder, or (c) reorder tweets for flow. By round 2–3 you'll have a solid draft with 1–2 clunky spots left.

> Bonus: use split screen in Edge to avoid context switching. For problem-solution content (good for sales), jot down the problems your audience faces and a few benefits of your solution as you go.

## 5/ Edit (Checklist or AI)

Run your draft through the QC prompt in `prompts/editing-qc-prompt.md`.

**Spend 80–90% of editing on the hook.** If nobody gets past it, nothing else matters.

## 6/ Write the Hook from the Swipe File

Match the hook to your structure. Pull from the swipe file (`../swipe-files/`). Some phrases repeat on purpose — **viral phrases**:
- *Once you see it, you can't unsee it.*
- *How X did this was wild.*
- *I spent X hours…*
- *The greatest / strangest / worst X in Y history.*

Process: pick a hook → copy its templated version → fill placeholders with AI (V1) → bracket the weak spots and note why → iterate with better words → get under 280 chars → pick an image matching the hook's emotion.

Full method in `writing-hooks.md`.

## 7/ Collect Media

Use snippets from saved articles plus Google Images. Don't overthink it — if you can't find something, leave it blank. Stay in split screen.

Where to look: Google · Perplexity · Google AI suggestions (screenshot) · articles · Google Images (quotes & infographics) · YouTube/Pinterest (only if client needs video; do images first).
