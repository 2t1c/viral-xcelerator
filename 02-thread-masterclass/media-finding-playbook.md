# Media Finding Masterclass Playbook

> "We are the Rolls-Royce of thread writing."

There are thousands of thread writers out there. We are the premium one. That positioning must refract through the content, and most visibly through the visuals.

This is the full media-finding process for Genius Threads and client threads.

## General Guidelines

**Setup**

- Use Microsoft Edge for media finding.
- Open Typefully in the sidebar so it stays on screen. Avoid context switches at all costs.

**Ratios and minimums**

- Image to video ratio for most threads: **8:2**.
- If instructed to clip from a video: **4:6**.
- Minimum **1 image for every 3 tweets**. No maximum.
- The more media, the better, as long as it's relevant.

**Relevance is the filter**

Relevance means prioritizing the media with the most intuitive connection to the tweet. It's subjective, so this is the working definition: the visual that feels most natural next to the idea.

**Sentiment alignment**

Media should maintain sentiment and alignment throughout a thread. A historical thread should not have a modern-designed picture in between unless it's truly related. Example: the Napoleon thread builds historical classiness with clear, well-designed, historically relevant images. A random reel clip in the middle would break the flow.

## Two Types of Images

1. **Informative images** — packed with information and context.
2. **Story-related images** — atmosphere and narrative support.

Lean into story-related images for historical and storytelling threads. But ALWAYS choose the informative image when one is available.

An informative image should provide novel information that's an **extension of the idea in the tweet**. Extension means it answers the **follow-up question** someone has after reading that tweet.

Example: the Napoleon thread ends on "the world without Napoleon," which mentally connects to the butterfly effect. Searching that surfaced a well-made, premium-looking butterfly effect graphic. That's the move: idea, mental connection, search the connected concept.

## Good Visual vs Bad Visual

| GOOD VISUAL | BAD VISUAL |
| --- | --- |
| Uniquely valuable information | Common information |
| Contextually related to the tweet | Far or no connection to the tweet |
| Professional, minimalist design | Blurry, outdated, looks like the 2010s |
| Clear and simple to comprehend | Overcomplicated, no clear message |

## Image Rules

- Always clear and easy to comprehend. Use an AI upscaler to sharpen if needed.
- Use the **keywords in the tweet** to determine the image.
- Use premium images: informative, scientific, artistic, or historical. We're building a professional, premium, luxury brand that resonates through visual quality.
- Prefer **16:9 ratio** to optimize skimming and scrolling. If using vertical images, pair two side by side so the tweet doesn't take up too much space.
- When in doubt, fall back to historical images and charts. Maintain relevancy.
- Example: for the McDonald's Monopoly thread, don't open with Ronald McDonald (wrong vibe). Open with the minimalist black-and-white shot with clear color contrast.

## Video Rules

For Genius Threads, we are slowly moving away from videos to focus on images only. Videos tend to decrease retention.

For client threads, unless repurposing from a specific video, keep the 8:2 ratio.

- All videos **under 60 seconds**. Average watch time is only 24 to 30 seconds.
- Minimum quality 720p. Exception: original clips closely related to the tweet.
- Original clips from the source are always preferred.
- Clipping from a longer video: use screen record to take the section needed.
- Clipping multiple clips from one video: use Opus Clip with the GTX template, set under 60s. Rearrange clips chronologically on Opus to see timeline and relevancy fast.
- Information rules mirror the image rules: single message, easy to understand, premium style.

## The Four Thread Types

Reference the thread type first to set expectations:

1. **Clipper** — multiple clips from one video. Clips are ~70% of visuals.
2. **Context-related** — some specific visuals but more flexibility as long as there's a historical or topical connection. *This is the most common type.*
3. **Video-heavy** — a curation of clips, one per tweet. Common in listicle and health/wellness threads.
4. **Movie** — everything as usual, but with clips from a related movie or series (Oppenheimer, The Queen's Gambit, The Vault). *Least common.*

## How to Find Visuals (Step by Step)

> "Everything about viral content nowadays can be boiled down to pattern recognition."

You can learn this entire process through analysis, pattern recognition, and reps.

1. Reference any custom instructions from Toan or Barsha.
2. Glance and skim the **reference thread type** to understand the expectation.
3. Read or skim the entire thread and identify the general vibe: historical, scientific, or storytelling. Most threads combine all three, but there's always a **central theme**. The theme tells you the right visual for each situation.
4. Go tweet by tweet. Find **2 to 3 keywords** per tweet: key ideas, key information, or unique insights. Usually the name of a concept, a technique, a location, or the action/interaction.
5. **Fill the entire thread with images first**, then move to video.
   - **Pinterest** is the go-to for artistic and historical pictures.
   - **Google Images** is for everything under the moon.
   - Alternate between the two for the best visuals.
6. Repeat the cycle on **YouTube and TikTok** for videos. Prioritize 16:9.
   - If you find a good informative video **over 6 minutes** on the topic (ideally one that's already gone viral, so it has social proof), screen-record multiple clips from it. Your job is 90% done. Add 1 or 2 extra shorts if needed.
   - If not, use YouTube's **4-minute filter** for shorter clips.
   - Use **Video DownloadHelper** (recommended) or Saveform to download faster. Ask Toan for a license.
7. Upload to the vacant tweets, or replace low-quality images with the videos.
8. Repeat until complete.

## Quality Bar

Two things matter at the end:

1. **Accuracy** of the information the visuals convey.
2. **Aesthetic** — the design must align with the premium brand and support retention.

Target: a full thread's media in 10 to 15 minutes once the pattern recognition kicks in.
