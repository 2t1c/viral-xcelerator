# Viral Hook Principles

The fundamentals behind every hook that performs. These run underneath every template — learn them and you can build or fix any hook.

## A hook is an offer

Think of it as a value equation (Hormozi): **(perceived value × likelihood of the outcome) ÷ (effort × time delay).** Every hook must answer one question before anything else:

> Why should this specific reader care, right now?

That question drives the entire hook. Everything else — specifics, numbers, authority — exists to raise perceived value and relevance around it.

## The five principles

1. **No assumption.** Assume the reader knows nothing and won't stop to figure out a reference. If they have to think, decode, or assume ("what's a Monopoly piece, and why do I care?"), they bounce. Keep the first line broad enough to catch the top-of-funnel scroller.
2. **Target primal feelings.** Fear, loss, revenge, envy, jealousy, status. Think about why news headlines are written the way they are — they go straight to the primal wiring. Our brains are the same as they were millions of years ago.
3. **Active voice + vivid scene.** Write so the reader can *picture* the action or scene. Vague is invisible.
4. **Viral phrases + adverbs to exaggerate and intensify.** Proven phrases carry little information but add stakes, rhythm, and the "this matters" feeling. (See `../swipe-files/`.)
5. **Prioritize and place.** Know the single most important fact and put it in the "prime real estate" — the one line carrying all the weight. This is where research earns its keep and where every word must fight for its life.

## What-Why-How (the universal spine)

Every viral hook — and every thread — follows **What → Why → How**:

- **What** — the one big idea (1–2 sentences). The hook.
- **Why** — why the reader should care / why it's relevant. *This is where the heavy lifting happens.* Either answer "why is this important?" extremely well, or dig into the pain (worst-case ↔ best-case spectrum).
- **How** — the steps, story, lessons, or patterns.

At hook scale it's three lines; at thread scale the hook is the What, the first tweets are the Why, and the payoff is the How. Establish importance **first**, then give information. Informative-but-emotionless middle lines are the most common reason a strong-looking hook underperforms.

## The Rule of One

One big idea, one target audience, one main takeaway / CTA. With so much noise, a hook that tries to do two things does neither. Even a skimmer should be able to see one clear idea and understand it.

## The contrast principle (especially business)

The bigger the gap, the more intriguing. Outcome vs. starting point, or outcome vs. effort: pizza boy → $1.3B company on $5; "the new Steve Jobs" buys a struggling fifth-tier club for $2.5M. The disconnect creates the "wait, that's weird — there must be something here" reaction that earns the click.

## Why business is the hardest niche

Health and psychology write about the reader's *own* pain, so relevance is automatic. History has a built-in storytelling audience. Business has no equally established native audience and is "boring" by default — you must manufacture interest and tie it to something people care about deeply. Nail business and you're among the top writers.

## Why psychology is the easiest niche

Psychology sits between health and business: you get health's powerful, universally-resonant keywords (anxiety, stress, depression — everyone has *some* mental-health issue) as a safety net, plus business's proven structure. Use the keywords as a wide net — hit one and the reader resonates; hit all three, better.

## Metrics that tell you a hook worked

- **Click-through rate** — the key signal (>5% is strong) and the main thing to optimize; it tells you how the content was perceived.
- **Retention** (>50%) — the other fundamental.
- **Bookmark-to-like ratio** — bookmarks higher than likes = high perceived value (people saving to act on it). Secondary: not every hook is built to be bookmarked; some are built to be opened (curiosity/open loop) or to spark comments (controversy).

> Note on the current algorithm: even a hook with >5% CTR and >50% retention no longer guarantees a boost. The algo is in flux. So go back to first principles — write a great hook, use enough distribution, and control what you can.

## The Risk-o-meter
"Everyone's an artist until rent is due." Threads are performative marketing, so creativity flexes with stakes:
- **Own pages = playground.** Test ideas and templates freely to learn what's proven.
- **Client work = low risk (1–2).** Start from a proven template, mold it, add the right keywords. You need results, so dial creativity down.
Before posting, ask: *how much does this need to perform, and what's the cost if it flops?*

## Negotiable vs Non-Negotiable (the house analogy)
A hook template is a house. **Non-negotiables = the frame/foundation** — remove them and it collapses. **Negotiables = the furniture** — the information you swap and personalize. Bracket the negotiables. Don't touch the non-negotiables; if you must, change only slightly (use a synonym). Keep the **first line broad** to catch top-of-funnel scrollers, and concentrate value in the one prime-real-estate line.

## The Inner-CTA
The **last line of the hook reframes the entire thread.** Same story, but "the pattern I witnessed blew my mind" instead of "here's how" changes how readers *perceive* the post — creating a category of one. To craft it: find the most interesting point in the thread, then ask the question that sits *just before* it; that framed question becomes the CTA line.

## Hierarchy of success
**Idea > Hook > Distribution > Body.** A good idea hands you the hook's components; the body's only job is to deliver on the promise the hook's last line makes.
