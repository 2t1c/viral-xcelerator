# Hiring and Scaling — From Operator to Team

Once your content machine runs and you are earning, the ceiling stops being your skill and starts being your time. Scaling past that means delegation, sharper analytics, and eventually a team.

Notion source: Viral Xcelerator Complete -> Service Based Business and Anonymous Virality.

---

## Scale in this order

1. **Systematize before you hire.** If the process only lives in your head, a hire cannot run it. The Idea Bank, Kanban, and swipe file (see `content-machine.md`) are what make the work handoff-ready.
2. **Delegate the time-sinks first.** Media finding, reporting, and scheduling are the highest-leverage tasks to hand off. They eat hours and need the least of your taste.
3. **Keep taste in-house longest.** Hooks and final editing are where your judgment matters most. Hand those off last, and only to someone you have trained on the frameworks.

## What to track as you grow

- **CTR and retention per thread**, so you know which hooks and structures actually travel.
- **Output per week** against your target, so batching stays honest.
- **Per-client economics** (revenue minus RT and delivery cost, divided by hours), so you scale the profitable work, not just the busy work.

## The agency question

Going from independent to agency trades higher pay-per-project for the ability to earn from other people's work. It is a real business with hiring, quality control, and client management on top of the writing.

Do it when demand outstrips your capacity and your process is documented well enough that someone else can hit your quality bar. Until then, a tight solo operation stacking two or three services (see `04-monetization/service-based.md`) often out-earns a messy agency.

## The trap to avoid

Scaling multiplies whatever you already have. Multiply a process that produces viral threads and you win. Multiply a process that is not working yet and you just create more mess, faster.

Get consistent results first. Then scale.
