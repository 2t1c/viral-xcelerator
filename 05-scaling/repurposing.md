# Repurposing — Write Once, Post Many Times

You only have to write a thread once. After that you can repurpose it endlessly.

Most followers will not remember your content after a week, and most of your audience missed it the first time. The timeline moves fast, and people rarely mind seeing valuable information again.

Notion source: Viral Xcelerator Complete -> Viral Thread Masterclass -> Content Repurposing: Best Practices.

---

## When to repurpose

Not all content deserves a second life. Repurpose threads that had solid engagement inside but lower reach than expected (strong CTR, above roughly 4%, but the hook did not travel).

## The process

**1. Hook transformation (9 out of 10 times, this is the whole job).**
The hook decides whether anyone sees the rest. Either use a completely different hook structure, or keep the structure and change the key wording.

Pull 10 candidate hooks from the swipe file, write them one by one, and see which fits best.

**2. Second-tweet optimization.**
Tweet 2 has to earn the read. Make it clearly answer "why should I care?" using a proven lead-in: extra credibility, your best insight, a controversial statement, or risk / FOMO.

**3. Thread-flow improvement.**
Find where people dropped off in the original. Strengthen transitions and open loops, insert a CTA before media, and tighten the flow with slippery-slope lines like "But here's where it gets interesting."

## Track the difference

When you repurpose, compare the new version against the original. You learn which hooks work for your audience and how small changes move performance. Repurposed content often beats the original once you apply these fixes.

## Keep it fresh

- Change the media (higher-quality images or video).
- Add insights you have gained since the original.
- Rephrase key points while keeping the core message.

## The 3-strike rule

If a thread does not perform after three repurposing attempts with different hooks and optimizations, retire it or transform it into something new. Keep tracking CTR and retention, and over time you build the instinct for turning underperformers into hits.
