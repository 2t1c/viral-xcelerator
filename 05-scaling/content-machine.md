# The Content Machine — Scaling Without Burnout

The target is volume (for many creators, up to 3 long-form posts a day). You cannot hit that on motivation or by "writing faster." Writing a thread start to finish in one sitting burns willpower and does not scale.

Stop treating a thread as one massive task. Build a system that breaks creation into small, repeatable steps.

Notion source: Viral Xcelerator Complete -> Anonymous Virality -> Building Your Content Machine.

---

## The 4 pillars

Get these in place and output stops being a willpower problem.

1. **A repeatable format.** Pick one proven structure and run it constantly. Stop reinventing the wheel, just fill in the blanks.
2. **A swipe file.** Never start from a blank page. Pull a proven hook, structure, and references, then adapt. Writing becomes assembly. See `swipe-files/`.
3. **Batching.** Never write one full thread a day. Write 8 hooks in a row, then 8 bodies, then find media for all 8. Context-switching kills speed, batching multiplies it.
4. **A Kanban workflow.** The glue. Every thread becomes a card that moves through clear stages.

## The two-part workspace

This is the system used to scale to roughly 100 threads a month with a small team.

**Part 1: the Idea Bank.** A database where ideas live before drafting, so you never sit down without a topic ready.
- Big Idea (the topic or angle)
- Source (podcast, book, viral post)
- Direction (the specific hook direction)
- Used? (a checkbox so you do not repeat yourself)
- Client (which page the post is for)

**Part 2: the Production Board (Kanban).** Every thread is a card whose only job is to move left to right:

`Drafting -> Media -> Internal Review -> External (client) Review -> Needs Review -> To Schedule -> Completed`

**Naming convention** so you can find any card at a glance:

> [Client Initial] + [Batch #] + [Position in Batch] + [Idea]

Example: `GTX1 - 1/8 - Marie Curie`.

## Why the board works

- **It kills overwhelm.** "Write a viral thread" is intimidating. "Draft the hook for this one card" is easy. You only ever face one small step.
- **It gives visual calm.** You see where every piece sits, so nothing falls through the cracks.

## The simpler alternative

Management can become its own trap. The moment admin becomes a task, you can end up managing content more than creating it.

So you have a choice. At the start, keep it simple and do everything straight from your Typefully scheduling calendar: look at the calendar, create the posts, schedule them.

Add the Notion Idea Bank and Kanban later, once you want more control over the pipeline (or once a team needs shared visibility).
